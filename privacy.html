Match! Privacy Policy

Effective November 25, 2026

Match! is a puzzle game made by Lucid Creative LLC ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the game keeps, what leaves your device, who receives it, and the choices you have.

The short version: your game is saved on your device only, not in the cloud. Your username and scores are shared only if you choose to become ranked by creating a rankings account in the game's Rankings tab. The iPhone version shows ads from Google, and purchases go through Apple. We don't use analytics, and we don't sell your information.

What stays on your device

To save your game, Match! keeps your progress on your device: the levels you've won, your best score and stars on each level, your coins, achievements and daily gifts, the worlds you've unlocked, your settings, and the username the game makes up for you when you first play (two nature words and two numbers). This is what lets you pick up where you left off. It is saved only on your device: the game doesn't put it in the cloud, and doesn't send it to us or to anyone else. The one exception is ranked play: if you choose to become ranked by creating a rankings account in the Rankings tab, your username and scores are shared through our server (see below).

The game also counts, on your device only, how it is used, such as which buttons are tapped, which levels are played and for how long. These counts are never sent anywhere.

Your iPhone's own backups (to iCloud or to a computer) include the game's data, as they do for every app; those are Apple's backups, and we never see them. Deleting the game deletes all of this, with one exception: an iPhone keeps your username (and, if you created a rankings account, the key that signs you in) in its keychain, so that it comes back if you install the game again. What you've bought is kept by Apple and comes back with Restore purchases.

Ads (iPhone)

Unless you buy Remove Ads, the game shows three kinds of ads from Google AdMob (Google LLC):

  • A banner ad at the bottom of the screen, which changes about once a minute.
  • An interstitial ad: a full-screen ad, often a short video, that can come up between levels, about once every seven to eight minutes of play. It never interrupts a level.
  • A rewarded ad: a short video you can choose to watch to keep playing after you lose. You never have to watch one, and coins work too.

To show the ads, measure them and prevent fraud, Google's ad software in the game collects information and sends it to Google. According to Google, this can include:

  • your device's internet (IP) address, and a rough location worked out from it (the game never asks for your location);
  • identifiers for your device: one that is tied to this app or to us, and your device's advertising identifier if you allow tracking (see below);
  • the ads you've seen and what you did with them, such as whether a video was watched or an ad was tapped;
  • other interactions, such as taps and when the app is opened;
  • performance data, such as how long the app takes to open, and crash logs.

Google uses this to show and measure ads and for analytics, as it describes in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services. We don't receive any of it about you: Google gives us only totals, such as how many ads were shown and what they earned. Tapping an ad opens the advertiser's page or the App Store, and their own privacy policies apply from there.

Tracking. Unless you've bought Remove Ads, the first time you open the game your iPhone asks whether Match! may track your activity across other companies' apps and websites. The game plays the same, and shows the same kinds of ads, whichever you choose. If you allow it, Google may use your device's advertising identifier to choose ads for you and to measure them across apps. If you don't, the advertising identifier isn't shared. You can change your answer at any time in your iPhone's Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.

Consent in Europe. If you're in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the game shows Google's consent message before any ad loads, so that you can choose whether Google may use your information for personalized ads. You can change your choice later under Privacy choices in the game's Settings.

Remove Ads. If you buy Remove Ads in the Shop, the game shows no ads, doesn't ask the tracking question and doesn't ask Google for any ad.

Purchases

Extra worlds and Remove Ads are bought through the App Store with your Apple Account. Apple takes the payment and tells the game what you own. The game never sees your card or payment details, and we receive nothing about you from a purchase except Apple's sales reports, which don't name you. Apple's own privacy policy applies to purchases.

The rankings (only with a rankings account)

The Rankings tab in the game shows your own best scores, which stay on your device. To be ranked against everyone and see where you rank in the world, you can create a rankings account in the Rankings tab (the Join button there). Your rankings account is kept on our server. Until you create one, the game sends nothing about you to our server.

If you create a rankings account, our server keeps:

  • the username you pick, and when you last changed it. Other players can see your username;
  • your scores: your total score (your best score on every level of every world added up, plus your best Endless score), which is where you rank in the world, and your best Endless score, each with when you made it and how many lines you cleared and quick moves you made. The server keeps your best scores for each week and of all time. Other players can see your scores and your place;
  • a random number for your account, and a fingerprint of a random key that your device makes and keeps, so that the server knows it's you;
  • how you sign in (see below);
  • when your account was made and when your device last used it;
  • any report you send about another player's name: which player, and why. Hiding a player is kept only on your device.

Signing in. To create a rankings account, you sign in with one of the ways the game offers: Game Center, Apple, Google or your email address. That lets you sign in the same way on another device and find your username and scores there.

  • Game Center, Apple or Google: we keep only the identifier that service gives us for your account. The game doesn't ask Apple or Google for your name or email address. If you're signed in to Game Center, the game may suggest your Game Center nickname as your username; it's sent to us only if you keep it.
  • Email: your address is sent to our email service, Resend, which emails you a six-digit code. Our server keeps only a fingerprint of your address, which can't be turned back into it, and a fingerprint of the code for ten minutes. Resend keeps a record of the email, including your address, for about 30 days.

Where it's kept. Our server and its database run on Cloudflare (Cloudflare, Inc.), a hosting company, which may handle the data in the United States and other countries. The game talks to the server only over an encrypted connection. Like any website, Cloudflare sees your device's internet (IP) address while it carries a request; our server doesn't keep it. The server also counts totals, such as how many sign-in emails go out each hour, to watch its limits; those counts say nothing about any player.

How long, and deleting it. Your account and scores are kept until the account is deleted. You can change your username in the game once a week. To have your account and everything on it deleted, email us your username at river@lucidcreative.associates and we'll delete it. Deleting the game from your device doesn't delete the account.

Other things you choose to send

  • Report a bug. Report a bug in Settings opens your own mail app with a message to us. It holds the version of the game, your device's model and system version, and the answers you type. You can read and change it, and nothing is sent unless you press Send. If you send it, we use your message and email address only to answer you and fix the problem.
  • Donate. The Donate button opens Trees for the Future's own donation page in Safari. You give on that page, under the charity's own privacy policy: the game takes no part in the payment, never sees it and takes no cut. Like any website, the charity's site can see your internet (IP) address.
  • Ratings. The question that asks you to rate the game is Apple's own, and ratings and reviews go to Apple.

Gift reminders

If you ask for them, your device shows a notification at about ten in the morning when your daily gift is ready. Your device makes these notifications by itself: nothing is sent to us or to anyone else, and the game uses no push service. Your iPhone asks for your permission only after you say yes in the game, and you can turn the reminders off at any time in the game's Settings or in your iPhone's Settings > Notifications.

What we don't do

We don't sell your information, and we don't use analytics services. The only third-party code in the game is Google's ad software (with Google's consent message), described above.

Children

Match! is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child under 13 has created a rankings account, write to us and we'll delete the account.

Your choices and rights

You can play without a rankings account, change your tracking answer in your iPhone's settings, change your consent choice where it applies, and buy Remove Ads to turn every ad off. Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what information we hold about you, to get a copy, to have it corrected or deleted, and to object to how it's used. Write to us and we'll answer within 30 days. For the information Google holds for ads, use Google's own tools, described in its policies.

If you're in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom: we use the rankings information to run the rankings you created an account for, and Google uses your information for personalized ads only with your consent. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll post the new version on this page with a new effective date.
